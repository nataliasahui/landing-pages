Privacy manifesto

Privacy is not a feature. It is the architecture.

We built Incode on a belief the industry rejected for years: that protecting people and protecting data are the same fight. Today we are proving it.

Ricardo Amper
Founder & CEO at Incode

The origin

We always knew privacy was the foundation. The world just wasn't ready to hear it.

From day one, Incode was built on a conviction that most of the industry considered naive: that digital trust and privacy are not opposing forces. They are the same thing. You cannot have real trust without real privacy. And you cannot deliver real privacy through half-measures, consent banners, or compliance checkboxes.

So we made a commitment that defined everything that followed: AI-first, AI-driven. No human reviewer in the standard verification pipeline, not as an optimization, not as a cost-saving measure, but as a foundational architectural decision. Every human reviewer is a privacy vulnerability. Every pair of eyes on a customer's biometric data is a point of exposure, a vector for breach. The industry accepted this as the cost of doing business. We refused.

Instead, we built proprietary AI from the ground up. Our own models. Our own research. Our own infrastructure. We invested years into building identity verification technology that could match and exceed what human reviewers did, without ever requiring a human to see a customer's face, document, or personal data. The result is a system that is not only more private but more accurate, faster, and more resilient than anything built on manual review.

We enable on-device biometric processing — keeping sensitive information inside the user's environment — and we are continuously expanding what that means. We minimize the routing of personal data to third parties, and where partners are involved, we hold them to the same privacy standards we hold ourselves to. World-class accuracy achieved through proprietary technology, not through the industry's standard playbook of collecting more data and hiring more reviewers.

The results validated the approach.

This is a brand film we created in our founding years. We are sharing it now as evidence of something simple: we did not arrive at privacy-first architecture because the market demanded it. We were founded on it.

But for years, the industry accepted a false tradeoff. Companies believed that to stop fraud, you had to sacrifice consumer privacy. That collaboration required data pooling. That defense meant exposure. The result was a world where your most sensitive data was copied, stored, brokered, and breached by the very systems that were supposed to protect you.

“Fraudsters collaborate. Defenders must as well. But collaboration cannot come at the cost of privacy. We were determined to solve this paradox.”

Ricardo Amper · Founder & CEO at Incode

The landscape finally shifted. Massive breaches. Regulatory pressure. The discovery of personal data stored by third parties for years without consent. The consequences of the old model became impossible to ignore. Companies began looking for a better way.

We had been working toward it all along. Not just on-device verification. Not just AI-driven review. We knew that the real unlock was network-level intelligence, a way for institutions to collaborate against fraud without ever pooling or exposing customer data. We had been investing deeply in solving this problem for years. We knew what it required. We went looking.

That search led us to Identiq.

The privacy-fraud paradox

The industry built fraud prevention on a foundation of data exposure. We refused to accept it.

For too long, the industry treated privacy and fraud prevention as a zero-sum game. Need to verify an identity? Collect and store the data. Need to detect a repeat fraudster? Share that data across institutions. Need to collaborate? Build a data lake and give everyone access.

This model created the very vulnerabilities it was supposed to solve. Every data lake is a target. Every third-party integration is a potential breach. Every shared dataset is a privacy violation waiting to happen.

We knew the answer had to be network-based. Institutions needed a way to share trust signals without sharing data. We had been investing deeply in this direction, working toward a model where collaboration and privacy were not at odds but were architecturally inseparable. What we needed was the cryptographic foundation to make that vision airtight.

AI-First, AI-Driven

No human reviewer in the standard verification pipeline, not as an optimization, but as an architectural principle. Proprietary AI built from the ground up replaces the single largest privacy and security vulnerability in identity verification.

On-Device Processing

We enable on-device biometric processing. We are continuously expanding what is possible. Sensitive information is kept as close to its source as the architecture allows — and that boundary keeps moving.

Minimal Third-Party Exposure

We minimize the flow of personal information to third parties. Where partners are involved, strict data governance applies. Data sovereignty stays with the organization and its customers.

The cryptographic breakthrough

We had the vision. We had been investing for years in privacy-preserving network intelligence. We went looking for the deepest cryptography, privacy engineering, and cybersecurity expertise in the market to bring that vision to life.

That search led us to Identiq. Nearly a decade of dedicated R&D. Over $50 million invested in patented privacy-enhancing technology. A team that had spent nearly a decade advancing the science of validating trust through cryptographic methods, without any party gaining access to another's personal data. By acquiring Identiq, we are combining our platform with a cryptographic layer built on nearly a decade of dedicated R&D and over $50 million in patented privacy-enhancing technology. The result is three capabilities the traditional model cannot deliver.

01

Collaboration Without Exposure

Companies share privacy-preserving trust signals rather than raw personal data. No central data lakes are created. No data brokerage occurs. Raw personal information stays under the strict control of each organization. The centralized attack surface that traditional collaboration created is eliminated by design.

02

Fraud Detection Without Surveillance

The network detects repeat fraud patterns and fraud ring patterns across institutions through privacy-preserving cryptographic analysis, without needing to know whose face or ID triggered any given signal. World-class fraud intelligence with no access to underlying personal data.

03

Less Friction for Legitimate Users

When a verification event maps to trusted behavioral patterns in the network, the system reduces friction in real time. Good users benefit from reduced friction without other institutions in the network learning the underlying identity. The network gets smarter with every verification while keeping every individual's data sealed.

“The acquisition of Identiq accelerates our ability to deliver privacy-preserving fraud collaboration at scale, without building a centralized database that becomes a breach target and a regulatory liability. Privacy is not a patch. It is the foundation.”

Ricardo Amper · Founder & CEO at Incode
